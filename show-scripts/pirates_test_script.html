ACT I
A ROCKY SEASHORE ON THE COAST OF CORNWALL
(In the distance is a calm sea, on which a schooner is lying at anchor. Groups of pirates are discovered drinking and playing cards. Frederic sits apart, despondent.)
OPENING CHORUS — Pour, O Pour the Pirate Sherry
ALL
Pour, oh pour the pirate sherry;
Fill, O fill the pirate glass;
And, to make us more than merry,
Let the pirate bumper pass.
SAMUEL
For today our pirate 'prentice
Rises from indenture freed;
Strong his arm, and keen his scent is
He's a pirate now indeed!
ALL
Here's good luck to Frederic's ventures!
Frederic's out of his indentures.
SAMUEL
Two and twenty, now he's rising,
And alone he's fit to fly,
Which we're bent on signalizing
With unusual revelry.
ALL
Here's good luck to Frederic's ventures!
Frederic's out of his indentures.
(The pirates drink Frederic’s health.)
PIRATE KING: Yes, Frederic, from to-day you rank as a full-blown member of our band.
FREDERIC: My friends, I thank you all, from my heart, for your kindly wishes. Would that I could repay them as they deserve!
PIRATE KING: What do you mean?
FREDERIC: To-day I am out of my indentures, and to-day I leave you for ever.
(General consternation.)
SAMUEL: But this is quite unaccountable; a keener hand at scuttling a Cunarder or cutting out a White Star never shipped a handspike.
FREDERIC: Yes, I have done my best for you. And why? It was my duty under my indentures, and I am the slave of duty.
PIRATE KING: Well, Frederic, if you conscientiously feel that it is your duty to destroy us, we cannot blame you.
FREDERIC: I will not abuse your confidence. I will attack you openly and according to the custom of honourable warfare.
(Ruth comes forward.)
RUTH: Oh, take me with you! I cannot bear to be left behind.
FREDERIC: Ruth, I will take you with me. But before I decide that you shall be my wife, I must see other women.
RUTH: Other women?
FREDERIC: Yes. You see, you are the only woman whose face I have seen since I was eight years old.
SONG — Oh, Better Far to Live and Die
PIRATE KING
Oh, better far to live and die
Under the brave black flag I fly,
Than play a sanctimonious part
With a pirate head and a pirate heart.
PIRATE KING
Away to the cheating world go you,
Where pirates all are well-to-do;
But I’ll be true to the song I sing,
And live and die a Pirate King!
ALL
For I am a Pirate King!
And it is, it is a glorious thing
To be a Pirate King!
(The pirates withdraw. Frederic and Ruth remain.)
FREDERIC: Ruth, tell me candidly and without reserve: compared with other women, are you beautiful?
RUTH: I have been told so, dear master.
FREDERIC: Ah, but lately?
RUTH: Oh, no; years and years ago.
(Voices of girls are heard approaching.)
CHORUS OF GIRLS — Climbing Over Rocky Mountain
GIRLS
Climbing over rocky mountain,
Skipping rivulet and fountain,
Passing where the willows quiver,
By the ever-rolling river,
Swollen with the summer rain;
Threading long and leafy mazes,
Dotted with unnumbered daisies,
Scaling rough and rugged passes,
Climb the hardy little lasses,
Till the bright sea-shore they gain!
(The daughters of Major-General Stanley enter, climbing down the rocks.)
EDITH: What a picturesque spot! I wonder where we are!
KATE: And I wonder where Papa is!
MABEL: Oh, he will be here presently.
(Frederic appears. The girls are startled.)
FREDERIC: Ladies, do not shrink from me. I am not a pirate — at least, not now.
MABEL: What are you, then?
FREDERIC: Until this morning I was a pirate apprentice. Now I am free, and I seek one who can overlook my former profession.
RECITATIVE & AIR — Poor Wandering One
MABEL
Poor wandering one!
Though thou hast surely strayed,
Take heart of grace,
Thy steps retrace,
Poor wandering one!
MABEL
Take heart, fair days will shine;
Take any heart — take mine!
GIRLS
Take heart, no danger lowers;
Take any heart — but ours!
(Mabel and Frederic move apart. The other girls tease them.)
FREDERIC: Mabel, from this moment I devote myself to you.
MABEL: And I to you, Frederic.
(The pirates enter stealthily behind the girls.)
ENSEMBLE — Here’s a First-Rate Opportunity
PIRATES
Now here's a first-rate opportunity
To get married with impunity,
And indulge in the felicity
Of unbounded domesticity.
GIRLS
We have missed our opportunity
Of escaping with impunity;
So farewell to the felicity
Of our maiden domesticity.
(Each pirate seizes a girl. Mabel comes forward.)
MABEL: Hold, monsters! Ere your pirate caravanserai proceeds against our will to wed us all, bear in mind that we are wards in chancery, and father is a Major-General!
SAMUEL: We’d better pause, or dangers may befall; their father is a Major-General.
ALL THE LADIES
Yes, yes, he is a Major-General!
(Major-General Stanley enters unnoticed above them.)
PATTER SONG — I Am the Very Model of a Modern Major-General
MAJOR-GENERAL
I am the very model of a modern Major-General,
I’ve information vegetable, animal, and mineral,
I know the kings of England, and I quote the fights historical
From Marathon to Waterloo, in order categorical.
MAJOR-GENERAL
I’m very well acquainted, too, with matters mathematical,
I understand equations, both the simple and quadratical,
About binomial theorem I’m teeming with a lot o’ news —
With many cheerful facts about the square of the hypotenuse.
ALL
With many cheerful facts about the square of the hypotenuse!
MAJOR-GENERAL
I’m very good at integral and differential calculus;
I know the scientific names of beings animalculous;
In short, in matters vegetable, animal, and mineral,
I am the very model of a modern Major-General.
ALL
In short, in matters vegetable, animal, and mineral,
He is the very model of a modern Major-General!
MAJOR-GENERAL: And now that I have introduced myself, I should like to have some idea of what is going on.
PIRATE KING: We intend to marry your daughters.
MAJOR-GENERAL: Oh, but you mustn’t do that. May I ask — what are you?
PIRATE KING: We are all single gentlemen.
EDITH: Papa, don’t believe them. They are pirates — the famous Pirates of Penzance!
MAJOR-GENERAL: The Pirates of Penzance? I have often heard of them.
MAJOR-GENERAL: I object to pirates as sons-in-law.
PIRATE KING: We object to Major-Generals as fathers-in-law. But we waive that point.
(The General thinks rapidly.)
MAJOR-GENERAL: Would you deliberately rob me of these the sole remaining props of my old age, and leave me unfriended, unprotected, and alone?
PIRATE KING: Well, yes; that’s the idea.
MAJOR-GENERAL: Have you ever known what it is to be an orphan?
(The pirates are deeply affected.)
PIRATE KING: Often!
MAJOR-GENERAL: I am an orphan boy!
(The pirates wipe away tears.)
PIRATE KING: You have appealed to the one soft place in our hearts. We do not attack orphans.
FREDERIC: But are you not deceiving them?
MAJOR-GENERAL: I am saving my daughters!
ACT I FINALE
PIRATES
Pray observe the magnanimity
We display to lace and dimity;
Never was such opportunity
To get married with impunity.
GIRLS
Let us thank them for their magnanimity,
They display to lace and dimity;
They have given up the felicity
Of unbounded domesticity.
(The General produces a British flag; the Pirate King displays the black flag. Tableau.)
ACT II
A RUINED CHAPEL ON MAJOR-GENERAL STANLEY’S ESTATE
(By moonlight. The Major-General sits surrounded by his daughters, troubled by the deception he practised on the pirates.)
MAJOR-GENERAL: Why do I sit here? To escape from the pirates’ clutches I described myself as an orphan, and I am no orphan.
FREDERIC: But you forget, sir. You only bought the property a year ago, and the stucco on your baronial castle is scarcely dry.
MAJOR-GENERAL: Frederic, in this chapel are ancestors. I don’t know whose ancestors they were, but I know whose ancestors they are.
FREDERIC: Be comforted. Had you not acted as you did, those reckless men would have married your large family on the spot.
MAJOR-GENERAL: At what time does your expedition march against these scoundrels?
FREDERIC: At eleven; and before midnight I hope to have swept them from the face of the earth.
(A body of policemen enters nervously, marching in single file.)
SONG — When the Foeman Bares His Steel
SERGEANT
When the foeman bares his steel —
POLICE
Tarantara! tarantara!
SERGEANT
We uncomfortable feel;
POLICE
Tarantara!
SERGEANT
And we find the wisest thing —
POLICE
Tarantara! tarantara!
SERGEANT
Is to slap our chests and sing —
POLICE
Tarantara!
POLICE
We go, we go,
Yes, forward on the foe!
DAUGHTERS
Go, ye heroes, go to glory,
Though ye die in combat gory,
Ye shall live in song and story —
Go to immortality!
(The police march away, then creep back anxiously, then march away again.)
FREDERIC: Now for the pirates!
(Pirate King and Ruth enter unexpectedly.)
PIRATE KING: Frederic!
FREDERIC: You!
RUTH: Yes, master. We have come to tell you something that may considerably affect your plans.
PIRATE KING: When you were apprenticed to us, your indentures bound you until your twenty-first birthday.
FREDERIC: Of course.
PIRATE KING: And you were born in leap-year, on the twenty-ninth of February.
FREDERIC: I have some dim recollection of the fact.
PIRATE KING: Then by birthdays you are only five and a little bit.
FREDERIC: This is most ingenious!
TRIO — A Most Ingenious Paradox
RUTH
You are twenty-one, going by birthdays,
Though counting by years, you are eighty-four;
PIRATE KING
So it is plain you must remain
A pirate apprentice for many years more.
FREDERIC
How quaint the ways of paradox!
At common sense she gaily mocks!
FREDERIC: Duty is before all. I must return to you.
PIRATE KING: Spoken like a true pirate!
FREDERIC: But first, as my duty demands truth, I must tell you that the Major-General deceived you. He is no orphan.
(The Pirate King is furious.)
PIRATE KING: What! I shall have his blood!
RUTH: And I shall help you!
(They hurry away. Mabel enters.)
MABEL: Frederic, why are you so pale?
FREDERIC: Mabel, I must leave you. My duty calls me back to my old comrades.
MABEL: But you are free!
FREDERIC: Not by the calendar of birthdays.
DUET — Stay, Frederic, Stay
MABEL
Stay, Frederic, stay!
They have no legal claim,
No shadow of a shame
Will fall upon thy name.
FREDERIC
Nay, Mabel, nay!
Tonight I quit these walls,
The thought my soul appalls,
But when stern duty calls,
I must obey.
(They part reluctantly.)
LATER THAT NIGHT — THE POLICE AWAIT THE PIRATES
(The policemen creep in, trying to be silent. Every movement makes an unfortunate noise.)
SONG — When a Felon’s Not Engaged in His Employment
SERGEANT
When a felon's not engaged in his employment,
Or maturing his felonious little plans,
His capacity for innocent enjoyment
Is just as great as any honest man's.
POLICE
Our feelings we with difficulty smother
When constabulary duty’s to be done;
Taking one consideration with another,
A policeman’s lot is not a happy one.
SERGEANT
When the enterprising burglar’s not a-burgling,
When the cut-throat isn’t occupied in crime,
He loves to hear the little brook a-gurgling,
And listen to the merry village chime.
POLICE
When the coster’s finished jumping on his mother,
He loves to lie a-basking in the sun;
Taking one consideration with another,
A policeman’s lot is not a happy one.
(The pirates enter. The police conceal themselves badly.)
PIRATE KING: Now then, you rascals, we seek the Major-General!
SAMUEL: No mercy!
(The Major-General appears in night attire.)
MAJOR-GENERAL: What is the meaning of this disturbance?
PIRATE KING: Your life shall pay for your deceit!
(The police rush forward.)
SERGEANT: Stay! We charge you yield, in Queen Victoria’s name!
(The pirates immediately kneel.)
PIRATE KING: We yield at once with humbled mien, because, with all our faults, we love our Queen.
POLICE
Yes, yes, with all their faults, they love their Queen.
MAJOR-GENERAL: Away with them, and place them at the bar!
RUTH: One moment! Let me tell you who they are. They are no members of the common throng; they are all noblemen who have gone wrong.
MAJOR-GENERAL: What, all noblemen?
PIRATE KING: Yes, all noblemen!
MAJOR-GENERAL: What, all?
PIRATE KING: Well, nearly all!
MAJOR-GENERAL: No Englishman unmoved that statement hears, because, with all our faults, we love our House of Peers!
(Everyone kneels.)
MAJOR-GENERAL: I pray you pardon me, ex-Pirate King. Resume your ranks and legislative duties, and take my daughters, all of whom are beauties!
(Each pirate takes a daughter.)
FINALE
RUTH
At length we are provided, with unusual facility,
To change piratic crime for dignified respectability.
PIRATE KING
Combined, I needn’t say, with the unparalleled felicity
Of what we have been longing for — unbounded domesticity!
MABEL
Tomorrow morning early we will quickly be parsonified —
Hymeneally coupled, conjugally matrimonified.
ALL
And this shall be accomplished by the doctor of divinity
Who happily resides in the immediate vicinity!
(General rejoicing. Curtain.)
